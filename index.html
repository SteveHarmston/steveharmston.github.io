What is AI Aware?

AI Aware is an AI detection platform that checks whether content was written, generated or altered by artificial intelligence. Unlike many detectors that handle only essays and articles, it covers four content types in one system: text, images, audio and video.

The need is easy to see. Generative tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Midjourney, Sora and ElevenLabs now produce material that looks and sounds convincing. For a university, a law firm, a newsroom or a bank, the question “did a person make this?” has become a real risk question, tied to academic integrity, fraud, brand trust and misinformation.

AI Aware reports each result with a confidence measure and highlights where AI-generated material sits inside a document or file, so reviewers can see how much and where, not just a verdict.

What AI Aware can detect

One platform, four detection modules. Each looks for signals specific to its medium.

AI-generated text

Essays, reports, emails and articles from major language models, scored with paragraph-level highlighting.

AI images

Pixel-level artefacts, repeated textures and metadata clues that point to image generators or manipulation.

Voice cloning and audio

Synthetic or cloned speech, identified through prosody, spectral traits and background-noise patterns.

Deepfake video

Facial inconsistencies, unnatural eye movement, lighting issues and frame-level anomalies in video.

How multi-model detection works

Single-algorithm detectors have a known weakness. A model trained on familiar data performs well on that data but can stumble on content from a new generator or an unfamiliar domain. Models built to handle novelty can, in turn, be less precise on well-known patterns.

AI Aware uses an ensemble approach: several detection models with different strengths are combined, and their outputs are blended into one assessment with a confidence score. The practical benefits are:

  • Resilience to edited content. Text that has been paraphrased, “humanised” or mixed with human writing is harder to hide from a portfolio of models.
  • Better coverage of new tools. Detection keeps up more easily as new generators appear.
  • Calibrated results. A confidence level helps reviewers decide when to escalate to a human check.

As with any detector, results are evidence rather than proof. The best practice is to treat a high score as a prompt for review, especially in high-stakes decisions about people.

Using an AI detector responsibly

Detection works best as one input in a wider process. Compare a flagged document with the author's earlier work, ask for drafts or version history, and give people a chance to explain before acting. For media, pair detection with provenance checks: who published it first, and does the source hold up?

Frequently asked questions

What is AI Aware?

AI Aware is a multi-modal detection platform that identifies AI-generated or manipulated text, images, audio and video, and reports a confidence measure for each result.

Can it detect deepfakes and voice clones?

Yes. Its video and audio modules look for signs of synthetic generation, including facial and lighting anomalies in video and cloned-voice artefacts in speech.

Is there a free trial?

Yes. AI Aware offers a free trial with no credit card needed, and larger organisations can request a broader evaluation.

Is any AI detector 100% accurate?

No. Detection is probabilistic. Use confidence scores to guide human review rather than as the sole basis for a decision.

Which AI tools can it spot?

It targets output from popular language, image, video and voice generators. Detection models are updated as new tools emerge.
